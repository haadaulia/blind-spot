Peer-set disclosure analysis

BlindSpot

A single filing can't reveal what it leaves out. BlindSpot reads a company against its peers and surfaces the risks they disclose but it doesn't.

How it works. For each company, BlindSpot pulls the Risk Factors section (Item 1A) of its latest annual report, uses an LLM to break it into individual risks (each kept with a verbatim quote), then groups similar risks across all the companies by meaning — not keywords. A risk theme that the peers share but the target never matches is flagged as a gap, and a second AI pass re-reads the target's full filing to throw out anything it actually discloses in different words.

Findings show relative disclosure — what a company says less about than its peers — never a claim about intent. Always confirm a flagged gap against the original filing before relying on it.

Reading the scores. Match strength (0–100) is how closely the target's nearest risk matches the peers' — by meaning, not wording. 65+ the target clearly covers it · 50–64 partial overlap · below 50 no real match, counted as a genuine gap. Scores are relative signals for this peer set, not absolute verdicts.
